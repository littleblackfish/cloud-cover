Étage

The cloud is a round patch of the layer. Azimuth and elevation place its centre in your sky (past 90° it crosses the zenith); lower it and the patch becomes a long oval far off.

Heights in

One cloud layer, parallel to the ground, seen by the observer at the centre. Everything is to scale: the observer's eye is 1.7 m up, and the dome stands on their horizon, 4.65 km away, so the ground inside it is all the ground they can see. Clouds sit at their real heights: low ones inside the dome, higher ones above it. They are drawn out to where the observer's line of sight, grazing the horizon, still reaches them: about 130 km for a 4,000 ft layer. Everything follows the Earth's curve. Cumuli or strati break the cloud up on the layer itself, so perspective squeezes them toward the horizon. Each étage has its own shade: low clouds greyest, high clouds nearly white. The view pulls back as the layer rises.
